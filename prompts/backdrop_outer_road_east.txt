looking east along the outer road past the official hall at the end of our scene: the rest of this stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its west end at eye level. Image 2's left end is nearest to us and its right end farthest away; its top edge is on our left and its bottom edge on our right. The road runs away from us to the horizon. On it, small and far off: two big two-wheeled family carts with high domed hoods of shaggy brown palm fibre on tall spoked wheels, the far one drawn by two oxen, with drivers, a rider and a few walkers. On the left of the road, nearest: a long hall on a raised floor with a dark blue cloth hanging along its eave, and in front of it a crowd of men standing round a seated man; a wheelwright bent over a big cart wheel; three pack donkeys going up a lane; then a huge, gnarled old tree, bare, with a thick mottled trunk and crooked branches (no leaves and no hanging willow strands), over low mat-awning sheds and an open-fronted tea house with tables and benches; far off on the left, at the end of the road, a bank path with a few tiny porters and four or five big cargo boats with arched mat roofs moored side by side against the bank, only a short reach of deep blue-green water drawn with fine ink ripple lines showing round them, the rest hidden behind low houses and bare trees. On the right of the road: the long grey tiled roofs of big halls one behind another, with a few bare trees.
