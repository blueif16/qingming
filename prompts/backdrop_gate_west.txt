The street runs straight away from us to the horizon, busy with small far-off figures: a big two-wheeled cart piled with roped sacks, two sedan chairs carried on poles, a man in a light robe riding a dark horse, porters with shoulder poles, pack donkeys, and a crowd round a storyteller at a crossroads. On the right of the street, nearest: the great two-storey wine house behind its tall flowered gateway of lashed poles (彩楼欢门) hung with flowers and red and blue streamers, a big parasol in front of it; then a butcher's shop, a crossroads with a side street opening away to the right, a corner shop, and an incense shop with a pole scaffold on its front and tall standing signboards. On the left of the street: low tiled houses half hidden by leafless trees, then a large two-storey inn with a long tiled roof and an upper gallery, and more tiled roofs one behind another. Far off where the street meets the horizon: low houses, a well curb and a few clumps of bamboo.
