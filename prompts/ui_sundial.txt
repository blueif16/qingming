Paint, in the style of image 1 (fine ink outlines, soft washes, muted natural colours, as a Song dynasty court painter would draw an object), a single round stone sundial face (an equatorial sundial, 日晷) seen exactly face-on, filling the whole square frame edge to edge.

The disc: pale grey limestone, true stone colour (not yellow, not sepia), lit evenly from the upper left. A slightly raised, rounded rim around the edge with a soft bevel and a few small chips and weathering marks. Inside the rim, two thin carved concentric grooves near the outer edge leave a ring band about one eighth of the radius wide; the rest of the face is a smooth, gently worn stone surface with faint mottling. At the exact centre, a small round bronze boss (about one twentieth of the disc's width) where the gnomon pin passes through.

Strictly no characters, no numbers, no hour marks, no ticks, no shadow lines, no text of any kind on the face: it must be blank stone so markings can be added later. No background scenery: the area outside the disc is plain flat white. No cast shadow outside the disc.
