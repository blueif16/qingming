looking east along the outer road from the end of the moat bridge: the next stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its west end at eye level. Image 2's left end is nearest to us and its right end farthest away; its top edge is on our left and its bottom edge on our right. The broad bare-earth road runs straight away from us to the horizon. On it, small and far off: a big two-wheeled covered cart with a barrel-vaulted roof of woven mat, seen from behind, its team of oxen ahead led by a bearded man on foot, a rider with a round fan coming the other way, porters with loads on shoulder poles, a few riders on donkeys and some walkers. On the left of the road, nearest: stalls under flat woven-mat awnings and two or three big dark parasols; behind them dark pines and old willows, and a bare open ground climbing away to a temple's tiled gate hall and a small open-fronted shop among pines; farther along the left, a plain earthen wall topped with crossed spikes, with a small tiled gate building in it and a few soldiers idling in front of it; then a fortune-teller's flat mat awning with narrow signboards hanging under it, beside a huge old pollard willow with a dark, gnarled, burled trunk and long bare whips. On the right of the road: the long grey tiled roofs of halls and a tea house, one behind another, a rounded thatched hut, a tall pole with a long striped cloth banner, and a few bare trees. The old willows are pollard willows before their leaves open: from each knobbly head a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff shoots springs upward and outward. They are NOT weeping willows: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no foliage.
