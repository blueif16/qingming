looking west along the river from the wharves at the west end of our scene: the next stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its east end at eye level. Image 2's right end is nearest to us and its left end farthest away; its top edge is on our right and its bottom edge on our left. All of it is far off across a stretch of white mist, so even its nearest boats, houses and people are small, the people's heads on the horizon. The river lies flat at the horizon as a thin sliver: a narrow flat band of blue-green with fine ink ripple lines, no deeper than a standing man is tall, never a wide sheet of water reaching toward the bottom of the picture; below it there is only white mist. In the middle of the picture, nearest of the big things, the great arched bridge stands over the river: one single arch with no piers, built of many long straight timbers laid side by side and lashed in overlapping courses into a broad, plain brown band that curves from bank to bank, square beam ends standing out of its face; its humped wooden deck has a railing of X-braced panels and is crowded with tiny people, small stalls under round parasols, a sedan chair, a rider on a brown horse and dark donkeys under big sacks; at its ends tall plain poles, each with two bars crossed in an X at its top. On the near side of the arch a big cargo boat lies across the river with its mast being lowered, many crewmen on its deck and roof poling with long poles, hauling ropes and waving; a cabin boat lies in the arch's shadow and a narrow empty skiff by its foot. On the right, the north bank, nearest: a small tavern with a table at the bridge's foot, a two-wheeled handcart being pulled up the bank, an open shed frame of posts and beams; beyond the bridge on that side, small and farther: a long open hall on posts with tables on its raised deck, shops with mat canopies and lattice fronts, and boats moored along that bank (a boat with its mast lowered, a long passenger boat with a continuous cabin of lattice windows, a boat with a dark mat cabin), and a few bare willows. On the left, the south bank, nearest: a long grey tiled building with an open front, a boat moored along the bank with men standing on its roof waving, low mat-roofed sheds with board walls under the arch, a big grey gable roof at the bridge's south foot; beyond the bridge on that side: a road with a crowd, porters, a single-wheeled barrow under a big hooped load, and the tavern's gate under a towering openwork scaffold of bare lashed poles with tiers of crossbars and spires of bound poles (no flowers, no streamers), its long tiled wing and its two-storey riverside hall with a railed balcony. Farthest, at the left end: a boat with an arched mat roof and men working one long sweep over its stern, and a masted boat towed by a long line to trackers walking bent forward on the far bank. The willows are pollard willows before their leaves open: from each knobbly head a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff bare shoots springs upward and outward. They are NOT weeping willows: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no foliage. No stone bridge, no pavilion on the bridge, no lake, no island, no sails, no rainbow colours, no hills, no city wall, no second bridge.
