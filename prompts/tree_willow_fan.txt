The tree: an early-spring willow (柳) as the scroll paints it, before its leaves have opened. A sturdy, slightly leaning trunk with knots and rough grey-brown bark forks into a few thick limbs; each limb ends in a knobbly head from which a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff, slender shoots springs upward and outward like the ribs of a fan, only the outermost shoots bending over a little at their tips. The fans overlap into a broad, airy crown with plenty of sky between the shoots. This is NOT a weeping willow: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no full foliage. The shoots carry only tiny swelling buds and the first specks of pale green, so the crown reads as fine red-brown lines under a faint pale-green haze, the way the shoots rise from the clubbed head of an old pollard willow.
Colours: grey-brown bark with darker knots; shoots red-brown; the buds and first tiny leaves pale willow green (about #a3b56e), sparse.

[1] Like the great willow in image 2: a thick trunk leaning a little to the left that forks about two-thirds of the way up into one long limb reaching out to the left and several limbs to the right, each with its fan of rising shoots; the crown a little wider than it is tall.
[2] A tall upright willow: a straight trunk that forks at mid-height into three limbs rising steeply, each crowned by a tall fan of shoots; the crown taller than it is wide and a little lopsided to the right.
[3] Two slender trunks rising together from one foot and parting gently, each ending in its own fan of rising shoots, with open sky between the two fans.
[4] A medium willow with a short, stout, twisting trunk that divides low into four spreading limbs, each with a fan of rising shoots; a rounded crown, its left side lower than its right.
