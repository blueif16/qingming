looking west along the north bank of the canal from the edge of the countryside: the canal's wharves, the next stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its east end at eye level. Image 2's right end is nearest to us and its left end farthest away; its top edge is on our right and its bottom edge on our left. All of it is far off across a stretch of white mist, so even its nearest shed, boats and people are small, the people's heads on the horizon. On the left of the picture the river runs away from us to the horizon: a flat, narrow band of blue-green water drawn with long flowing ink current lines and small curling crests, no deeper than a standing man is tall, never a wide sheet of water reaching toward the bottom of the picture; on its far side there is no bank, only white mist. Moored one behind another along the river's right-hand bank, their bows pointing away from us: nearest, a small plain planked boat with a big rudder; then a broad, deep-laden grain boat with a woven-mat hood over its bow and heavy coiled cables piled on its after deck, a ladder leaning down from it to the small boat; then a second big grain boat with two overlapping woven-mat roofs over a small deckhouse at its bow, its cargo under nets of crossing ropes, a high stern gallery and a big rudder, a cleated gangplank running up the bank from its stern; farther on, a big timber boat with a gable-roofed fore cabin, a long flat-roofed plank cabin with a ladder against its side and round bundles along its roof, and a barrel-vaulted mat hood aft; farthest, a big river boat with a broad dark arched mat canopy over its foredeck and a long rounded mat cabin roof heaped with casks and bundles. No mast is up and there are no sails. On the right, the bare earth of the wharf rises gently from the boats to a row of open sheds. Nearest on the right: a long open eating shed under a big hipped thatched roof, with a lower thatched skirt roof along its front, standing on many plain posts over tables and benches with seated customers; at its near end a round-faced man in a light robe sits on a stool holding the lead rope of a small dark donkey. Just behind it stands the fire-watch tower: a platform of horizontal brick courses, about as high as a house's eaves, with a straight wooden stair and hand-rail climbing its side, carrying a small open pavilion of four posts under a pyramidal tiled roof with wide flaring eaves and a round finial; nobody is in it. A few bare willows and bare trees stand behind the shed. On the open wharf in front of the sheds: porters bent under big grain sacks carried up from the boats, a heap of sacks with a stout man seated on it keeping count, two men shaking hands. Farther along on the right: a long open shed under a tiled roof with a hatched mat curtain hanging from its eave and tables beneath it; a house with a small balustraded loft under its gable; a lower open eating shed with a front gable and a flat awning, and a cord of small pennants running from its roof to the top of two tall poles leaning together into an A. Behind them, a tall open frame of long poles and two leaning ladders stands free in an empty yard, and at the far right, along the top of the bank, a long single-storey shop open along its whole front, its shelves stacked with paper goods, a tall narrow signboard on a post at its end. Then, on the bank beside the river, a huge old pollard willow with a thick, twisted, knotted trunk leaning out over the water to a massive knobbed head; a raised plank walkway with a slatted railing runs along the bank beside it, and behind it a big open eating shed with a huge tiled roof, a flat woven-mat awning on posts over its tables and a big round mat parasol. Farthest, where the bank meets the horizon: a white-walled house with a hipped tiled roof, a closed warehouse with tall lattice doors on a plank-faced platform, and a long riverside hall with a row of big open windows under one huge tiled roof, an open shop with a big dark curtain drawn up at one end beside it; along the far right edge, a back street of small tiled sheds on posts with tables, a round-topped cart and a horse with its head down. The willows are pollard willows before their leaves open: from each knobbly head a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff bare shoots springs upward and outward. They are NOT weeping willows: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no foliage. Leave out the huge dark willow with a burled trunk that fills image 2's bottom right corner: it stands on our side of the edge and is not part of this view; there only the small boat and the water show.
