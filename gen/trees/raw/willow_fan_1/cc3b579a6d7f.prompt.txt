Image 1 shows the target art style. Images 2 and 3 are details of the Qingming scroll (清明上河图) showing the kind of tree to paint: take from them only the tree's form, its branching and how its shoots or needles are drawn, and ignore everything else in them (people, roofs, umbrellas, the silk's yellow colour).

Paint ONE single tree in exactly image 1's style: hand-inked dark-brown outlines and linework, light hatching on the trunk, flat muted painted colour fills, crisp clean edges, every shoot drawn as a clear confident stroke.

The tree: an early-spring willow (柳) as the scroll paints it, before its leaves have opened. A sturdy, slightly leaning trunk with knots and rough grey-brown bark forks into a few thick limbs; each limb ends in a knobbly head from which a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff, slender shoots springs upward and outward like the ribs of a fan, only the outermost shoots bending over a little at their tips. The fans overlap into a broad, airy crown with plenty of sky between the shoots. This is NOT a weeping willow: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no full foliage. The shoots carry only tiny swelling buds and the first specks of pale green, so the crown reads as fine red-brown lines under a faint pale-green haze, the way the shoots rise from the clubbed head of an old pollard willow.
Colours: grey-brown bark with darker knots; shoots red-brown; the buds and first tiny leaves pale willow green (about #a3b56e), sparse.

This one: Like the great willow in image 2: a thick trunk leaning a little to the left that forks about two-thirds of the way up into one long limb reaching out to the left and several limbs to the right, each with its fan of rising shoots; the crown a little wider than it is tall.

The whole tree from the foot of the trunk to the top of the crown, seen from the front at eye level, centred, filling the height of the picture with a small even margin, nothing cut off by the picture's edge. The trunk ends cleanly at its foot with a slight root flare, standing on nothing.

True natural early-spring daylight colours of the real tree, never the yellowed, darkened silk of the scroll.

Isolated on a solid plain pure white background that fills the whole picture edge to edge: no ground, no grass, no soil patch, no shadow, no mist or glow, no other trees, no other objects, no text, no seal, no border, no frame.
