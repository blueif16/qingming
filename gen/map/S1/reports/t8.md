## Lessons (exact text for the skill)

- **Read faint small animals in a gridless crop of the whole group first.** Make a zoom-2–3 crop of the whole group,
  contrast-normalised (sharp `normalise` 0.5–99.5 plus a light `sharpen`), with ticks only on its border and no grid
  over the figures. Then take points on a gridded crop. At zoom 4–5 the silk's weave breaks the strokes into noise,
  and which end is the head is lost.
- **Don't take a vignette's direction from its title or the restyle.** S1's charcoal train is 赶驴入城 ("into the
  city", westward), and the restyle walks it west. On the strokes, D4's pricked ears and D2's and D3's lowered heads
  are at their east ends. Record each animal's facing from its own head (ears, and which end the neck rises from).
  Say the conflict in the group's note, and give it to the lead, because it decides the walking routine.
- **The restyle reads loaded panniers as people:** two bundles with lumpy tops become two men in hats. This is one
  source of its invented crowds round pack animals.
- **A pale grey trunk behind a dark tree is a separate, further tree, not a limb** (S1 36465). The restyle paints
  a second straight trunk there too. Its base is usually hidden. Record it with `est` and the visible stretch in
  `note`.
- **Test a lone thin line by following it.** A silk crease runs straight through everything. A rope or pole stops
  at things. A line from a moored boat's end to a bridge post is the boat's mooring rope: it is part of the boat,
  and its far end is named by the structure's ID.
