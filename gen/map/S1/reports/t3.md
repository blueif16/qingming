## Lessons (exact text for the skill, "Mapping a section")

- **Brightness profiles settle trunk feet that the eye merges.** Where several big trunks stand foot to foot (S1's 隋堤烟柳), take the mean grey in 10 px bins along scan rows every 30–40 px of v across the feet (a 20-line sharp script). The outlines show as dips and open silk as peaks. It decided three of four willow feet in S1 t3 where zoom-2/3 crops and the restyle disagreed by 30–60 px.
- **Long straight vertical lines through crowns and ground are creases or seams in the silk, not trunks or posts** (S1: u 31236–31240, v 20–460; 31480–31505, v 410–760). The restyle turns them into trunks. Check that a "trunk" ends somewhere and has two outlines.
- **A setup's "figure" from a zoom 0.4–1.2 look can be a prop:** the S1 shed's "figure and a table or tub" is a jar with a tied cover and a basin. Read every hinted figure at zoom 3–4 before counting it.
- **Read the structures agent's part before calling an ambiguous mass.** structures_w had independently seen a leaning willow trunk in front of the hut's fence, which made one reading of the hollow bole likely. A tile agent can read `gen/map/<S>/parts/structures_*.json` when it exists, and should say so in its note.
- **In the countryside the restyle's trunk count is right and its crowns are not.** It added green weeping willows and a tiled house in the crowns. Every trunk it drew in u 31100–32100 matched a trunk on the scan. Use it to tell which trunk is which, never for feet or crowns.
- **Interlaced willow crowns get `est` extremes.** Assign whips to the tree whose pollard head they converge on, and give a measured `top` only where one crown stands clear against the silk.
