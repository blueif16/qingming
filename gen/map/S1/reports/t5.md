## Lessons (exact text for the skill)

- **Trace bare trunks on a red-channel crop.** Where the silk is dark and the ink faint, extract the scan's red
  channel, normalise it and enlarge 1.7–2.4×: ink goes dark, the yellow silk light, and the painter's pale-filled
  trunks with two dark contour lines read as pale bands. Zoom 3–4 of the natural-colour scan only blurs on S1's silk.
  Count a grove at three levels: a horizontal strip through the trunks (count the bands), the feet zone (roots and
  where each band meets the ground), and the crown band (follow each band up to its head); a trunk that stops at a
  roof line is a tree behind the house with a placeholder base.
- **Two contour lines are one trunk.** A pair of parallel dark lines 20–25 px apart with a pale middle is one trunk,
  not two thin ones; two stems from one root spread are one tree (record the stems in `what`).
- **The restyle draws trunks well and people badly in groves.** In S1's farmhouse grove it put the trunks 20–40 px west
  of the strokes but in the right number; every person and animal it added there was a trunk foot, a brush clump or a
  shed.
