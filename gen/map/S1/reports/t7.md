## Lessons (exact text for the skill `scroll-section`, "Mapping a section")

- **Pale far trees on dark silk: blur before you stretch.** At zoom 2–3 the silk weave is as dark as a pale trunk's
  outline, and plain normalising or CLAHE amplifies the weave. A greyscale crop blurred by about 1.5 px, then
  normalised and given a gain of about 1.3 (sharp: `.greyscale().blur(1.6).normalise({lower:2,upper:98}).linear(1.3,-30)`),
  at zoom 1.4–2.4 shows each trunk as two outline strokes, so the trunks can be counted and their feet found.
- **Crossing trunks: follow each outline from its foot.** Where bare trees cross (S1 35850–36150), trace each trunk's
  pair of outlines from the ground up, at zoom 1.5 over the whole height. Record one tree per foot. Name the stems
  that leave one foot in its `what`. Where two trunks meet and one carries on, give both readings of which top belongs
  to which foot in the notes. The feet don't change, only the heights.
- **Ask the structures agent's part for the bank before choosing a tree's support.** A tree on a stream bank can stand on the top,
  the face or the foot of the bank (S1: its foot on the waterline). Compare the foot's v with the bank's `bank_top` and
  `bank_foot` at that u, and give the support (`bank slope`) and both v in the note.
- **The restyle's people in tree tangles are usually bank strokes.** A pale patch of ground between trunks, with a dark
  contour stroke, is drawn as a man (S1 35940). Check for a head, a robe and feet at zoom 3 before you record anything.
