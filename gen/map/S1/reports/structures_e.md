## Lessons (exact text for the skill, "Mapping a section")

- **Read ink-wash ground by its visible faces.** A dark wash band you can see faces the viewer, so the ground steps
  down toward the viewer across it. Record it as `bank_top` (its upper edge) and `bank_foot` (its lower edge), higher
  side north. Tongue-shaped wash strokes pointing into blank silk are spits or land tongues. (S1, 2026-10-12.)
- **Anchor water on what stands in it.** Use bridge posts' feet and a lying boat. Extend the water only as far as the
  same blank silk runs, and record the rest as `water` with `conf: low` and both readings in the note. The restyle
  dries every wash band, so it cannot help here. (S1.)
- **A stream's support comes from a deck that meets the banks level.** Take the deck's edge and a post's foot straight
  below it. (S1 stream: 52–58 px, H ≈ −0.7 m.)
- **In a farmstead, a band of crossed short hatching under a smoother roof wash is a brushwood fence or hedge**
  hiding the walls, not more thatch. Put the eave at the change of texture. (S1.)
- **Roofs seen gable-on recede.** A small gable vent at the top with the thatch sweeping down both sides means the
  house's long axis runs back into the picture. Give its placeholder footprint more depth than its width suggests,
  and say so. (S1.)
- **When the structures unit is split in two, write the join piece early and read the neighbour's part before
  finishing.** Split your bands the way the neighbour split them, and list the ID pairs at the join. (S1: structures_w
  copied the road's join points, and the bank pairs at 33000–33150 are listed for the merge.)
- **The countryside may have no ground-level receding edge.** When nothing at ground level recedes, say so and let
  the frame carry the seam knot. Don't fit a dx to eaves or a curving path. (S1 east half: one weak candidate in
  4700 px.)
