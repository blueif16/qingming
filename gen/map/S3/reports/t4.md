## Lessons (exact text for the skill `scroll-section`, "Mapping a section")

- **Read the neighbours' parts before you finish.** They land while you work. List their entities within 150 px of your
  edges with a short script over `gen/map/<S>/parts/*.json`, and settle each straddler in both parts' notes. On S3,
  t3 confirmed the 串车's front from t4's note. t5 dropped its copies of an awning and a figure once t4's part landed,
  and joined t4's donkey to its barrow group by ID.
- **A group may list only its own part's IDs** (`check` fails on a member it can't find). Make the group with your own
  members, and name the neighbour's members in the group's `note` for the merge.
- **A barrow's front is the end away from its handles.** If handles rest near the ground and men lean into the load
  from that end, it is the rear. Give reading B (shafts, front at that end) in the note, with the point that would change
  the owner.
- **Read an animal's head end from its tack.** The halter rope ties at the head, a breeching strap lies over the rump.
  Where the tile restyle is misregistered, the overview names animals better: on S3 the tile restyle made a donkey seen
  from behind into an ox, and the overview kept it.
- **Goods on a stall are their own props** on `table top: <id>`, as on S6. A mat canopy on posts is a tile prop (S4's
  mat awning). Say in its note that structures may read it as a shed, so the merge keeps one.
- **A corner-based prop's main point is its `sw` corner** (`primary()` takes the first ground point). Choose the
  placeholder corners knowing which tile that puts the prop in, and say so.
- **To read a blurred cluster, crop the scan at zoom 1.2–1.5 with a sparse grid (100), then take the points at zoom 3.**
  At zoom 3–6 the scan's own resolution blurs dense figures into texture, and the whole figures are easier to see at
  low zoom.
