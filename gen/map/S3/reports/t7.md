## Lessons (exact text for the skill)

- "At the Rainbow Bridge's crown (S3) the scan's strokes blur into texture at zoom 3. Read dense crowds at zoom 1.6–2.4
  on a contrast-stretched crop (sharp `normalise` 1–99 % plus a light `sharpen`), then confirm single heads at zoom 3–4.
  Put a dot on every head you count, re-read the dotted crop for unmarked heads, and only then place feet."
- "People leaning over a railing: put their hidden feet a step behind it, not on the deck's painted edge. A man standing
  right at a painted 1.4 m parapet would come out about 200 px tall where the crowd is about 140. Measure the handrail
  at the posts' tops first, and compare with the bridge agent's lines."
- "Before finishing, read the neighbours' parts if they have landed, and drop your copy of anything they already own
  across the seam (S3 t7: a broad-hatted man, a tree crown, a man at the railing, and a shed recorded by structures_e)."
- "A tall pole with a crossed bar at its top, rising behind a crowd, can be the far-side end post of a bridge, not a
  mast or a parasol's pole (S3: u ≈ 19172, X at v 20–50, foot hidden behind the deck's crowd)."
- "A horse in profile in a crowd: find its head, its tail and the rider's seat line before deciding how many animals
  there are (S3's horse is ≈ 175 px from head to tail, 1.25 × a standing man). A separate head beyond the tail is a
  second animal with `conf: low` and both readings."
- "Decide whether a parasol or awning stands on a bridge deck or on the far bank by its painted size: at deck scale
  (figures ≈ 140 px) a parasol's canopy sits about 190 px above its base, and a far-bank one is painted smaller."
