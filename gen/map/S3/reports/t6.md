## Lessons (exact text for the skill, "Mapping a section")

- **Read a crowd at zoom 2 on a contrast-stretched crop, then check with numbered markers.** The scan has about
  1800 px for the silk's height, so zoom 3 and above shows grain, not strokes. A small sharp script (extract →
  normalise 1–99 % → resize → composite an SVG of numbered head and foot markers from a working list) re-renders a
  window in seconds. Iterate until every marker sits on its stroke, then sweep once more with head markers only and
  look for unmarked heads.
- **Take the deck/ground boundary from the bridge agent before writing supports.** On S3 the deck's south foot is a
  level line in the picture (v ≈ 1090), so stalls and people far west of the painted railing still stand on the
  ramp. A tile that finishes first marks its supports as provisional.
- **Read the neighbours' "left to" lists before finishing.** t5 found a third dark donkey shape and a basket on the
  boundary that I had folded into other entities.
- **Decide a loaded animal's facing from its legs, not its load.** A hind leg's hock points toward the tail, a
  foreleg's knee points forward, and the tail is thin with a tuft. When a neighbour disagrees, write both readings in
  the note.
- **A stall recorded with footprint corners has the sw corner as its main point** (the tool's first ground point).
  Name the ID after that u, and expect the west neighbour to own it by the rule. Say in both reports which ID to keep.
