## Lessons (exact text for the skill `scroll-section`, "Mapping a section")

> - **Willow heads with hidden trunks.** Where bare willow whips fan from a knob or a convergence point and no trunk
>   stroke shows (behind a bigger tree's crown, a roof or a gate), record one tree per fan centre. Give it a `base`
>   `est` on the nearest readable ground behind the occluder, `conf: low`, and the knob's point in `note`. Never take
>   the trunk from the restyle, which invents light trunks. On S4b t4 the whips over u 14100–14650 had two such
>   centres and a third in t3's range.
> - **Give the sign of every receding edge you measure on furniture.** On S4b the tea house's tables and benches slope
>   down-right (dv/du +0.38 to +0.75, receding up-left), while the shop 400 px up the silk runs up-right (−0.57) and
>   S4's ground edges ran −1.4 to −2.0. A tile agent's furniture edges are often the only ground-level evidence in an
>   open hall, and a flipped sign must reach the frame agent before one knot is fitted across it.
> - **Name a support for every raised floor** (`tea house floor`) with its step's two stroke points, rather than
>   `ground` by default. A tile brief's support list names only the floors the lead foresaw.
> - **Report the gap between the brief's density estimate and the strokes** (S4b t4: 20–25 estimated, 10 painted).
>   This stops the merge and the second pass from hunting for people who were never painted.
> - **A side-by-side zoom settles the restyle's additions faster than the scan alone.** Look for the restyle's
>   figure, then ask whether the scan has a head stroke there. On S4b the bending figure at the tea house's east end
>   was a post and braces, and the drum beside the rail figure was three loose strokes.
