## Lessons (exact text for the skill)

- "Read faint silk with contrast-stretched crops, not the plain overlay. Above zoom 3 the plain overlay of the 38414 px scan shows
  only the weave. A crop through sharp's `normalise({lower:1, upper:99})` with lanczos resizing, and a faint grid, reads heads and
  feet at zoom 4–7 and layout at zoom 2–2.5. (S2 t11's `crop.mjs` is a 30-line example.)"
- "When the setup's notes say the restyle 'makes X an animal', open that tile's check sheet (`gen/sections/<folder>/check/
  t_rRcC.jpg`) and find it before reading the scan. On S2 the 'leaping dog' was the dark-tipped cross-bars of a ladder on the
  bank, and a 'flying bird' was a bent porter's head. The restyle invents animals from small dark marks."
- "Two heads over one body: test which head the body belongs to by the bare silk under each head. A head with nothing under it
  belongs to a body leaning toward it. Record the other head as a second, `conf: low` figure with both readings, never merge them
  silently."
- "Trees whose feet hide behind roofs: give the base `est` where the roof hides the trunk. Say in the note how far north the true foot
  could be (the shed's depth), because the base decides the tree's depth in the world. Only the knobbed head and its whips
  show above the ridge for S2's pollarded willows behind the sheds."
- "Report furniture edges with their sign. On S2's east bank the tables' sides slope down to the east (+0.7 to +1.1), the opposite of
  S4's receding edges. A frame fitted without them would put the depth axis the wrong way."
