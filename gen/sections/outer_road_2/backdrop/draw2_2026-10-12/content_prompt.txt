looking east along the river from the moorings at the end of our scene: the next stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its west end at eye level. Image 2's left end is nearest to us and its right end farthest away; its top edge is on our left and its bottom edge on our right. All of it is far off across a stretch of white mist, so even its nearest hall, boats and people are small, the people's heads on the horizon. The river and the road lie flat at the horizon as thin slivers: the water is a narrow flat band of blue-green with fine ink ripple lines, no deeper than a standing man is tall, never a wide sheet of water reaching toward the bottom of the picture; below it there is only white mist. The river runs away from us on the left of the picture; on the right, along its near bank, a dusty road runs beside the water to the foot of the bridge. A little right of the middle of the picture, farthest away, the great arched bridge stands over the river: one single arch with no piers, built of many long straight timbers laid side by side and lashed in overlapping courses into a broad, plain brown band that curves from bank to bank, square beam ends standing out of its face; the humped wooden deck on top has a railing of X-braced panels and is crowded with tiny people, stalls, a sedan chair and a rider; at its ends tall plain poles, each with two bars crossed in an X at its top. Just beyond the arch, seen through it and above its deck, a big cargo boat lies across the river with its mast being lowered, many crewmen on its deck poling and hauling ropes; a cabin boat lies in the arch's shadow. They close the view: beyond the bridge only a few low tiled roofs and bare trees, no open water running on to the horizon. On the river, nearer to us: a boat with a big arched mat roof coming toward us, five men working one long sweep oar over its stern and three men at its high bow; a heavily laden boat with a tall mast, towed by a long line from its masthead to five trackers walking bent forward in single file on a bank path on the far (left) shore. Along the far (left) bank toward the bridge: a row of moored boats (a very long passenger boat with a continuous cabin of lattice windows, a boat with a mat awning over its bow, a long low boat with its mast lowered onto a crossed crutch, a boat with a dark arched mat cabin), and behind them small open-fronted shops with tables under mat canopies, a long open hall on short stilts with tables on its raised deck, and a few bare pollard willows. On the right, nearest: the long two-storey riverside hall of a tavern, a big grey tiled roof over an upper balcony with a railing where men sit eating at a table, bare trees in front of it; then the tavern's long tiled wing and, over its gate, a towering openwork scaffold of bare lashed poles with tiers of crossbars, three tapering spires of bound poles and fans of poles splaying out like a crown (no flowers, no streamers); from it a long bar runs to a tall pole, and a long pale banner with three dark vertical bands hangs from the bar; a flat dark mat canopy over stalls of jars and bottles; a cargo boat moored at the bank with men unloading jars and bundles; on the road before the bridge a single-wheeled barrow under a huge domed load bound with ropes, porters with shoulder poles, dark donkeys under huge pale sacks, big round parasols over stalls, and a crowd growing thicker toward the bridge's ramp; a big grey tiled gable roof at the bridge's foot. The willows are pollard willows before their leaves open: from each knobbly head a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff bare shoots springs upward and outward. They are NOT weeping willows: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no foliage. No stone bridge, no pavilion on the bridge, no lake, no island, no sails, no rainbow colours, no hills, no city wall, no second bridge.
