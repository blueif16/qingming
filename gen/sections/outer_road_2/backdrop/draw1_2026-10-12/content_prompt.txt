looking east along the river from the moorings at the end of our scene: the next stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its west end at eye level. Image 2's left end is nearest to us and its right end farthest away; its top edge is on our left and its bottom edge on our right. On the left of the picture the river runs away from us, a band of blue-green water drawn with fine ink ripple lines, narrowing toward the bridge; on the right, along its near bank, a dusty road runs away beside the water to the foot of the bridge. Far off, a little right of the middle of the picture, where river and road meet the horizon: the great arched bridge, one single arch of plain weathered brown timber with no piers, its arch a broad band of many parallel curved beams springing from both banks, the humped deck on top with a railing of X-braced panels, crowded with tiny people, stalls, a sedan chair and a rider; a tall plain pole with two bars crossed at its top stands at each end. Beside the arch a big cargo boat lies across the river lowering its mast, many crewmen poling and hauling on it, and a cabin boat lies under the arch. Beyond the bridge the view ends: a few low tiled roofs and bare trees on both banks, nothing farther. On the river, nearer: a boat with a big arched mat roof coming toward us, five men working a long sweep oar at its stern and three men at its high bow; a heavily laden boat with a tall mast, towed by a line from its masthead to five trackers walking bent forward in single file up a bank path on the far shore. Along the far (left) bank toward the bridge: a row of moored boats (a very long passenger boat with a continuous cabin of lattice windows, a boat with a mat awning over its bow, a long low boat with its mast lowered onto a crossed crutch, a boat with a dark arched mat cabin), and behind them small open-fronted shops with tables under mat canopies, a long open hall on short stilts with tables on its raised deck, and a few bare pollard willows. On the right, nearest: the long two-storey riverside hall of a tavern, a big grey tiled roof over an upper balcony with a railing where men sit eating at a table, with bare trees in front of it; then the tavern's long tiled wing and, over its gate, a towering openwork scaffold of bare lashed poles with tiers of crossbars, three tapering spires of bound poles and fans of poles splaying out like a crown (no flowers, no streamers); from it a long bar runs to a tall pole, and a long pale banner with three dark vertical bands hangs from the bar; a flat dark mat canopy over stalls of jars and bottles; a cargo boat moored at the bank with men unloading jars and bundles; on the open ground before the bridge a single-wheeled barrow under a huge domed load bound with ropes, porters with shoulder poles, dark donkeys under huge pale sacks, big round parasols over stalls, and a crowd growing thicker toward the bridge's ramp; a big grey tiled gable roof at the bridge's foot. The willows are pollard willows before their leaves open: from each knobbly head a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff shoots springs upward and outward. They are NOT weeping willows: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no foliage. The river does not widen into a lake and does not run on to the horizon past the bridge; no sails, no rainbow colours, no hills, no city wall, no second bridge.
