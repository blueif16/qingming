looking west along the road from the tavern at the west end of our scene: the previous stretch of the scroll (image 2), seen from its east end at eye level. Image 2's right end is nearest to us and its left end farthest away; its top edge is on our right and its bottom edge on our left. Everything in this picture is far away across a stretch of white mist: its nearest things stand some seventy paces from us, so even the nearest people are small, about a twentieth of the image height, with their heads on the horizon and their feet just under it. The painted ground under the horizon is no deeper than those people are tall; the road and the water are flat slivers of it, already narrow at their near ends, never a wide wedge opening toward the bottom of the image. Below that sliver of ground there is only white mist. In the middle of the picture the broad bare-earth road runs straight away from us to the horizon. On the right, nearest, the river ends at a low bank: five boats lie moored side by side across the flat water, their far ends against the bank and their near ends toward us: the biggest, a long timber hull with a hip-roofed cabin, a man resting on its cabin roof and ropes from its far end to a short mooring post; a boat with an arched mat awning on hoops, a boatman sitting under it; a long boat heaped with woven mats and bales under a dark barrel-shaped mat roof; a long boat with a big dark mat roof, a planked deck cabin and a little deckhouse with lattice windows; a broad round-sterned boat under a barrel mat roof; and farther right, small, a cabin boat moored along the far bank. The water runs off the right end into white mist. Behind the boats, on the far right along the top: low single-storey tiled houses with open fronts, tables, benches and chairs under their eaves, a long faint shed, a small house with a gateway, a tiled house set askew at the top of the bank, a small open shed with a woven mat roof on posts, a gnarled stump at the bank's edge, two men talking on the bank. On the right of the road, nearest: an old willow with a massive, gnarled, knotted trunk forking into two leaders, one long level limb reaching out over the road, fans of bare shoots; a low tiled house behind it with a screen of reeds; then the tea house: a big open single-storey hall on a low platform, its big grey tiled gable roof facing the road and a flat mat canopy over its front, men sitting at tables on chairs, stools and benches; an open mat-roofed stall with a long low table and a slatted box; a big round ribbed mat parasol behind its roof; behind the tea house the fans of bare shoots of two or three willow heads. Farther along the right of the road: a big bare two-stemmed elm with gnarled, knot-holed bark and crooked budding twigs, no leaves; the wheelwright's yard: a narrow tiled shed with a lean-to on posts, a big spoked cart wheel lying flat on the ground with the wheelwright bending over its rim with a mallet, a man in a wide dark hat watching, a tall hooped frame of bent poles with a big round basket hanging inside it, a low pile of squared timbers, long poles leaning on the shed, a table heaped with goods and a big round shallow basket; behind it, up on the right, a street with a long row of open eating houses with tables and benches, three pack mules with dark loads on their pack saddles and their muleteer, a two-tier stall of round wares. Farthest on the right of the road: a crowd of a dozen men in dark caps and long robes standing in two rows round a man seated on the ground over goods spread on a mat, under a big sloping tiled roof; two old bare gnarled trees with dark twig clumps over them and a pair of tall straight bare elms; an open shop hall with tables, customers and a signboard, and the open hall of an office with a man at work on its raised floor. On the road, small and far off: a big two-wheeled family cart under a huge shaggy roof of palm thatch, parked at the road's right side with no team; a dark-roofed sedan chair set down beside it with a young man at its poles; a saddled dark horse with no rider, men tending it, a small dark donkey; a second palm-thatched cart coming toward us, pulled by a light ox in the shafts and a big light lead ox with long curved horns, a driver walking beside them and a small light horse at its rear held by a man in a big black hat. On the left, all along the road's left side, nearest: the backs of a row of big halls, heavy grey tiled roofs one behind another (a big hall whose ridge rises toward us, a long hall with a straight ridge, a hall with a small gable and a hanging-fish ornament, a big hall with a railed gable), their walls hidden under their eaves; farthest on the left, an open shop under a long lattice eave with tables, a long bench and a tall barrel. The willows are pollard willows before their leaves open: from each knobbly head a half-open fan of long, straight, stiff bare shoots springs upward and outward. They are NOT weeping willows: no hanging curtains, no drooping strands, no foliage. No bridge, no city gate or wall at the end of the road, no tower, no pagoda, no stone quay, no lake, no island, no sails, no hills, no green trees.
