PART: crab-claw twigs with their tight buds
WHAT: clusters of bare elm twigs
EACH: Each one is a single cluster of dark, angular "crab-claw" twigs: short, bent, knobbly twigs that hook and turn like claws, forking again and again from one short stub at the bottom centre of its quarter, drawn in dark ink, the twigs dark brown-grey and darkest toward their tips, ending in small dark clumps of tight buds (dark brown, never pink or red). No leaves, no blossom. The cluster is ragged and see-through, with white showing between the twigs, and fills most of its quarter.
SHAPES: (1) a tall cluster rising upward; (2) a wide, spreading cluster; (3) a cluster reaching out to the left; (4) a cluster reaching out to the right.
