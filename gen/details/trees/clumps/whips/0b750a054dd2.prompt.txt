Image 1 shows the target art style. Image 2 is a tree painted in that style; copy exactly how its long straight whips with their tiny new leaves are drawn: the same stroke, line weight, colours and spacing.

Paint a sheet of four separate sprays of willow whips, arranged in a 2 by 2 grid, each one alone in its own quarter of the picture with clear white space all round it, never touching another one or the picture's edge.

Each one is a single dense spray of many long, straight, slender red-brown whips that all spring from one small point at the bottom centre of its quarter and rise upward and outward like a fountain, the longest arching over a little at their tips, with tiny pale fresh-green new leaves (about #8fa35f) just opening along them. The whips are single unbranched lines from that point; there is no knob, no branch and no trunk below the point. The spray fills most of its quarter.

The four differ only in shape: (1) a tall upright fountain, narrow at the bottom and wide at the top; (2) a wide dome of whips arching out on both sides; (3) a spray sweeping far out to the left; (4) a spray sweeping far out to the right.

True natural early-spring daylight colours of the real tree, never the yellowed silk of the scroll. Isolated on a solid plain pure white background that fills the whole picture edge to edge: no trunk, no branch, no ground, no shadow, no text, no seal, no grid lines, no border.
