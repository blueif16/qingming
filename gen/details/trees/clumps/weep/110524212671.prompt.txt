Image 1 shows the target art style. Image 2 is a tree painted in that style; copy exactly how its hanging bare whips with their first specks of green are drawn: the same stroke, line weight, colours and spacing.

Paint a sheet of four separate curtains of hanging willow whips, arranged in a 2 by 2 grid, each one alone in its own quarter of the picture with clear white space all round it, never touching another one or the picture's edge.

Each one is a single loose curtain of long, thin, flexible bare whips that all hang from one small point at the TOP centre of its quarter and fall straight down like strings, spreading a little as they fall, yellow-green to red-brown (about #a6a46a), with tiny swelling buds and a few first specks of pale willow green (about #a3b56e). See-through, with plenty of white between the strands. There is no branch above the point. The curtain fills most of the height of its quarter.

The four differ only in shape: (1) a narrow curtain falling straight; (2) a wide curtain spreading as it falls; (3) a curtain swaying a little to the left; (4) a curtain swaying a little to the right.

True natural early-spring daylight colours of the real tree, never the yellowed silk of the scroll. Isolated on a solid plain pure white background that fills the whole picture edge to edge: no trunk, no branch, no ground, no shadow, no text, no seal, no grid lines, no border.
