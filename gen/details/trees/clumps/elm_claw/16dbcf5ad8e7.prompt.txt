Image 1 shows the target art style. Image 2 is a tree painted in that style; copy exactly how its crab-claw twigs with their tight buds are drawn: the same stroke, line weight, colours and spacing.

Paint a sheet of four separate clusters of bare elm twigs, arranged in a 2 by 2 grid, each one alone in its own quarter of the picture with clear white space all round it, never touching another one or the picture's edge.

Each one is a single cluster of dark, angular "crab-claw" twigs: short, bent, knobbly twigs that hook and turn like claws, forking again and again from one short stub at the bottom centre of its quarter, drawn in dark ink, the twigs dark brown-grey and darkest toward their tips, ending in small dark clumps of tight buds (dark brown, never pink or red). No leaves, no blossom. The cluster is ragged and see-through, with white showing between the twigs, and fills most of its quarter.

The four differ only in shape: (1) a tall cluster rising upward; (2) a wide, spreading cluster; (3) a cluster reaching out to the left; (4) a cluster reaching out to the right.

True natural early-spring daylight colours of the real tree, never the yellowed silk of the scroll. Isolated on a solid plain pure white background that fills the whole picture edge to edge: no trunk, no branch, no ground, no shadow, no text, no seal, no grid lines, no border.
