Image 1 shows the target art style. Image 2 is a tree painted in that style; copy exactly how its long straight shoots with their tiny buds are drawn: the same stroke, line weight, colours and spacing.

Paint a sheet of four separate fans of willow shoots, arranged in a 2 by 2 grid, each one alone in its own quarter of the picture with clear white space all round it, never touching another one or the picture's edge.

Each one is a single fan of many long, straight, stiff, slender red-brown shoots that all spring from one small point at the bottom centre of its quarter and rise upward and outward like the ribs of a half-open folding fan, arching over a little at their tips, with tiny pale fresh-green buds (about #8fa35f) along them. The shoots are single unbranched lines from that point; there is no knob, no branch and no trunk below the point. The fan fills most of its quarter.

The four differ only in shape: (1) a tall narrow upright fan; (2) a wide half-open fan spreading almost level at its sides; (3) a fan leaning to the left; (4) a fan leaning to the right.

True natural early-spring daylight colours of the real tree, never the yellowed silk of the scroll. Isolated on a solid plain pure white background that fills the whole picture edge to edge: no trunk, no branch, no ground, no shadow, no text, no seal, no grid lines, no border.
